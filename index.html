Executive summary

Three things to take into the room

All figures come from Dis-Chem's GA4 property. “Before” is 14 Jun – 17 Aug 2026 and “after” is 18 Aug – 21 Oct 2026, 65 days each. The after window starts where the data of the last report ended. Weekly GA4 data puts the fix in the week of 23 Aug, five days later.

1

The mobile checkout fix worked

Mobile begin checkout → purchase is up +4.0 pts (36.9% to 40.9%), and the weekly data shows a lasting step from the week of 23 Aug, with shipping → payment rising from 50% to 54% (the shipping-step gain is on iPhone, while Android did not improve there). The gain holds on an equal 28-day comparison. Desktop is not confirmed.

2

Overall conversion is flat because of where new traffic lands

Users to purchase is 0.59% against 0.61%. Cross-network, Direct and Organic Search improved +20%, +22% and +27% in revenue per session, but Paid Social sessions more than doubled and 40% of them land on one promotion page that earns R0.01 per session for them.

3

The next gains are at shipping, in the cart and in a few faults

46% of mobile users who add shipping never reach payment, and in a sample of delivery-step sessions most stalled there. Clarity also shows a JavaScript error, mostly on iPhone browsers, in about 2% of sessions that rarely reach checkout. GA4 shows Android is now the weaker device at the shipping step (50.4% against 56.7% on iPhone). Each +1 point at the main steps is worth roughly R325K to R912K per 65 days.

Why this is not a promotion effectPurchases per checkout starter averaged 38.8% in the three promotion weeks and 39.3% in the five weeks after, and shipping → payment held its new level in both. A promotion would lift checkout completion only while it ran. Desktop did not show the gain, which a site-wide promotion would. We estimate the fix itself added about R3.1M over 23 Aug – 17 Oct (a range of R2.5M to R3.4M, or 5% to 6% of revenue), as set out in the Insight Report.
Payment → purchase cannot be read from this viewAdd payment users and purchase users are almost identical every week, so add_payment_info appears to fire at about the same time as purchase. The 76% → 91% payment → purchase figure may therefore partly reflect event tracking. We use shipping → payment and purchase ÷ begin checkout as the reliable measures.

Clarity's summary of the recordingsClarity's AI summary of the same sample points to three things: repeated JavaScript errors during browsing and checkout, clicks on the store selection prompt ("Please make your selection") that do not progress on the Collect in Store option, and a few payment declines or dead clicks late in the flow. What five individual sessions addClarity's summaries of five long mobile sessions (a small, anecdotal set) add three leads. First, updating the cart repeatedly triggered a JavaScript error (checkOnlyInts is not defined) in one session, and errors also appeared as another shopper moved between product pages and the delivery step. Second, shoppers move back and forth between shopping and the delivery step, adding items, removing items and logging in part-way through. Some of the losses we count at the shipping step may be shoppers carrying on shopping, so a clearer way to keep the basket and delivery choice while browsing is worth testing. Third, one shopper tried to check out with Collect in Store and Capitec payment, then left to browse, which fits the store selection and payment issues in the Clarity summary. All three need more recordings to confirm.
The iPhone skew is the sample, not the funnel84% of the sampled sessions were on iOS and 65% on Safari, against 34% for Safari across all Clarity sessions. GA4 shows iPhone and Android shoppers reach the shipping step in almost equal numbers (53.9K and 53.5K users), so Android shoppers are not dropping out earlier. The skew most likely comes from the late-evening sample, and the sample should be read as direction only.
